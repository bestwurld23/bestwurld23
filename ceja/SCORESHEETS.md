## What the three scoresheets say when read together

**Each has a different centre of gravity.** Climate Works splits evenly between
capacity and quality. Navigator is capacity-weighted (42) and its single
largest question is staffing all six key program areas (10). The Hub is
quality-weighted (51) and its single largest question anywhere in the three
NOFOs is **"the training you would deliver" at 11 points**.

**Where the Foundation's assets actually earn points:**

| Asset | Climate Works | Navigator | Hub |
|---|---|---|---|
| NCCER / NABCEP curriculum, staff instructors | Q6 = 8 | — | **Q10 = 11** |
| Employer partnerships (Knobelsdorff, Common Energy) | Q4 = 3 | Q13 = 5 | **Q6 = 8** |
| Registered apprenticeship + contractor partnerships | **Q5 = 8** | Q13 (shared) | not scored |
| Englewood roots, equity lens | Q15 = 5 | Q5 = 5 | Q5 = 4 |
| Barrier reduction partner network | Q11 = 4 | Q11 = 4 | Q14 = 6 |

Two things stand out. The equity-roots question is worth only 4-5 points
everywhere — Englewood is a real asset but it is not where the points are, and
the application should not lean on it as though it were. And the Hub rewards
training delivery and employer partnerships with 19 points combined, while
asking nothing about registered apprenticeship relationships, which is the one
thing the Foundation does not yet have.
